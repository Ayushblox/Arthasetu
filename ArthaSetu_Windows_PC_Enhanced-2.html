A realistic roadmap

  • Year 1: Fix the basics. One-window business registration online, faster payments between banks, and digital invoices for small firms.
  • Year 2: Open credit to small firms. Use cash-flow data (wallet and QR sales) to lend, rather than only land as collateral.
  • Year 3: Turn remittances into investment. Diaspora bonds, savings products for returning workers, and training for those who come back.
  • Year 4 and on: Export what Nepal is good at. Hydropower, tourism services, IT outsourcing, herbs, tea and handicrafts, backed by trade finance.

Do

  • Keep business and home money separate.
  • Issue a bill for every sale.
  • Reconcile your cash and wallet balance weekly.
  • Ask customers for feedback and repeat orders.

Don't

  • Borrow for daily costs without a repayment plan.
  • Mix personal loans into business accounts.
  • Skip tax registration when you pass the threshold.
  • Trust unknown links or calls asking for your PIN.

Common questions

What is the difference between PAN and VAT?

PAN is your tax identity number. VAT registration is needed once your turnover passes the current threshold, and then you charge and report 13% VAT.

Can I get a loan without land as collateral?

Sometimes. Some lenders look at cash flow, guarantees or group lending, and cooperatives often ask for less paperwork. Clean sales records make every option easier.

Sole proprietorship or company?

A sole proprietorship is simple and cheap for small shops. A company gives limited liability and more credibility with banks and partners.

How much should I save before starting?

A common rule is to hold several months of running costs in reserve, so slow sales do not force you to close.

Where can I check the latest rules?

Ask the offices listed above or your ward or municipality. Fees, thresholds and schemes change over time.
